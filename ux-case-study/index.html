Redesigning the AI-Powered Health Dashboard

How we improved patient engagement by 45% through intelligent personalization and trust-building design patterns.

Problem Statement

Patients using the existing health dashboard reported low engagement (12% weekly active rate) and expressed distrust in AI-generated health recommendations. The legacy interface surfaced insights without context, leaving users confused about why specific actions were suggested.

With the rise of AI-native health tools in 2025-26, users expected transparency, personalization, and an empathetic tone — none of which the current product delivered.

Key Insights

Three core themes emerged from our research:

01

Trust Requires Transparency

Users need to understand WHY AI recommends something before they act on it. "Show me the evidence" was the #1 request.

02

Personalisation ≠ Complexity

Users want tailored insights but reject interfaces that feel overwhelming. Progressive disclosure is key.

03

Emotional Context Matters

Health data is deeply personal. The tone, timing, and framing of AI messages directly impacts engagement and trust.

04

Design Process

We followed a double-diamond approach adapted for AI product design, with an emphasis on prototyping with real AI outputs early.

Week 1–2: Explore

Stakeholder interviews, research synthesis, "How Might We" workshop, problem framing

Week 3–4: Define & Ideate

User flows, information architecture, concept sketches, AI prompt prototyping

Week 5–6: Design & Test

High-fidelity mockups, usability testing (3 rounds), accessibility audit

Week 7–8: Deliver & Measure

Dev handoff, design QA, A/B tests, launch monitoring, iteration

Solution — Final Design

The redesigned dashboard centers on three pillars: Explainable AI, Progressive Personalization, and Empathetic Communication. Every AI recommendation now includes a confidence level, source references, and a plain-language explanation.

06

AI Design Decisions

How we approached AI-specific UX challenges: trust calibration, graceful failure states, and keeping humans in control.

🛡️

Trust Calibration

Confidence meters on every AI insight. Users see how certain the model is before acting on recommendations.

🔄

Human-in-the-Loop

Users can override, correct, or dismiss AI suggestions. Every override improves model accuracy over time.

⚠️

Graceful Degradation

When AI confidence is low or data insufficient, the UI shifts to manual mode rather than showing unreliable predictions.

📖

Explainability Layer

"Why this recommendation?" expandable panel on every AI-generated card, citing data sources and reasoning chain.

07

Accessibility & Inclusive Design

We followed WCAG 2.2 AA standards, tested with screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA), conducted sessions with users of varying abilities, and ensured the AI communication adapts reading level based on user preferences.

09

Reflections & Learnings

What worked: Prototyping with real AI outputs early in the process revealed edge cases we would have missed otherwise. Involving clinicians as design partners was invaluable.

What I'd do differently: Start A/B testing earlier in the process rather than waiting for a polished prototype. Ship-and-learn beats perfect-and-launch.

Next steps: Expand the explainability patterns to the mobile app, introduce voice-based AI interactions, and explore multimodal health data visualisation.
